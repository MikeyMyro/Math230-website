Top 10 Four-Seam Fastballs of 2021

There is no single way to build an elite fastball. This ranking starts with the result — Run Value per 100 pitches — and then looks at the velocity, movement, results, and surrounding arsenal that helped make each pitch effective.

The Ranking

Ranking Method

The list uses Run Value per 100 four-seam fastballs among pitchers who threw at least 500 four-seamers in 2021.

Total Run Value naturally rewards workload because a pitcher who throws a pitch more often has more opportunities to accumulate value. Run Value per 100 puts every pitcher on the same scale and focuses more directly on how valuable the fastball was each time it was thrown.

The countdown starts at No. 10 and works toward the most valuable four-seam fastball in the qualified group.

Individual Fastball Spotlights

Each fastball is broken down through the same areas:

Velocity → Pitch Shape → Relative Movement → Effectiveness

The numbers show what the pitch did. The 2021 context helps explain why it worked.

10: Feyereisen, J.P. — RV/100 = 1.8

2021 Context
Feyereisen is probably the clearest example on this list of shape overpowering ordinary velocity. He finished 2021 with a 2.73 ERA, but what made the fastball stand out was the amount of ride and spin he created without needing upper-90s velocity. It is a perfect example of a fastball playing much harder than the radar gun suggests.

Final Ranking

After looking at each fastball individually, the full ranking puts the differences in Run Value per 100 into perspective.

What I like most about this group is how little the pitches actually have in common. Some dominate through pure velocity. Others play above their velocity because of movement. Others become elite because the rest of the arsenal makes the fastball much harder to anticipate.

Final Takeaway

There is no single blueprint for an elite fastball.

deGrom shows what happens when historic starter velocity is paired with elite command and a devastating slider. Hendriks combines velocity with almost absurd strike throwing. Feyereisen shows that extreme ride can allow ordinary velocity to play much harder. Lynn shows how multiple versions of the fastball can work together. Peralta and Richards show how strongly the rest of an arsenal can influence how a fastball plays.

That is probably the biggest thing I take away from the entire list. The best fastball is not necessarily the hardest fastball or the pitch with the most IVB or the most unusual horizontal movement.

The best fastballs find a way to make multiple characteristics work together.
